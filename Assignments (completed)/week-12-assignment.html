Although I am not really familiar with the state of Tennessee, several patterns in the choropleth maps align with broader demographic and geographic trends that are consistent with what is known about the state. It is unsurprising that urban school districts such as Shelby County (Memphis) and Metro Nashville show higher percentages of Black and Hispanic teachers. These areas have historically had more racially diverse populations, which is often reflected in both the student and educator demographics.

It is concerning (though unsurprising) to see that many rural and suburban districts across east and middle Tennessee have very low percentages of teachers of color. This likely reflects both the racial makeup of these communities and systemic challenges in attracting and retaining a diverse teacher workforce in rural settings.
